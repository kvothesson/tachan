How to transfer photos from Android to PC wirelessly

There are several ways to move photos and videos from an Android phone to a Windows computer. None is best for everything: it depends on whether you have a cable, how many photos there are, and whether you mind them going through the internet.

1. With a USB cable

Plug the phone into the PC, pull down the notification shade and choose File transfer. The phone shows up in File Explorer, and the photos are in the DCIM folder.

It's the fastest way to move hundreds of photos at once. The downside: you need the cable, and many cables that come with chargers only charge and don't carry data.

2. Through the cloud

Google Photos, Google Drive, or emailing or messaging them to yourself. Nothing to install on the computer: you open the website and download them.

The downside: they go up to the internet and then you have to download them, so it's slow with many photos. And some apps lower the quality.

3. With Google's or Microsoft's apps

Quick Share, from Google, and Phone Link, from Microsoft, transfer photos wirelessly. Both need their app on Windows and, depending on the case, signing in with your account.

4. With LocalSend

It's free, open source and goes over your wifi with no account. It also works with iPhone and Mac. You open the app on both devices and pick who to send to.

5. With Tachán

The difference is how it arrives: a small notice appears on your computer without taking you away from what you were doing, and the photo is already copied. Press Ctrl+V to paste it into a chat, an email or a document, or drag it from the notice. It's also saved in Downloads.
